This file plans the board. It used to open by naming "a single hardcoded ring in
=src/map.rs=" — a 32x24 grid, =TILE = 32.0=, four corner waypoints in =RING= —
and to set out to move that into an asset. It moved: =map-001= is =DONE=, and
what is left here is what the ring never had.

The board is *data*. =src/map.rs= is a loader and a validator over
=assets/maps/*.ron=, and the board that ships is the real one, Green TD v36.0
(=assets/maps/green_td_v36.ron=, `wc3maps 153802`, =map-015=): 96x96 tiles of
128 world units, ten lanes from ten spawn points, merging into one funnel and
ending at a single goal region (=END=) at the bottom centre. A creep walks its
lane and, at the end of it, **leaks** — it leaves the match and costs a life
([[file:02-simulation.org][02-simulation.org]] =sim-005=, =sim-006=). The module stays shared by
client and server and carries no authority, and the board is hashed into the
protocol version, because two peers on different boards would disagree about
every leak ([[file:09-networking.org][09-networking.org]] =net-001=).

What the ring did not have, and so what is left here: the shared funnel that
makes "closest to the goal" mean the same thing on every lane (=map-002=), the
per-cell build mask (=map-006=), a terrain palette for a board drawn from its own
zones (=map-007=), a second board and the registry that lists it (=map-013=), the
picker that chooses between them (=map-009=), heading for a creep drawn along a
lane (=map-012=), a doodad layer (=map-010=) and the tiled ground pass
(=map-014=).

* Map data model and loading

** DONE [#A] Load the board from a data asset instead of the =RING= constant   :@map:
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:         map-001
   :EFFORT:     1w
   :END:
   =map.rs= hardcoded the grid (=GRID_W = 32=, =GRID_H = 24=, =TILE = 32.0=) and
   the path as the four-corner =RING= array, so a second map could not exist and
   nothing outside =map.rs= could vary the field. All of it moved into a
   serialisable board definition, loaded and validated before any plugin exists.

   - Files: =src/map.rs=, =assets/maps/green_td_v36.ron=
   - Acceptance:
     - [x] A map asset declares the tile grid, the lanes, the goal and the named zones, and is loaded at match setup (found-004).
     - [x] =GRID_W=, =GRID_H=, =TILE= and =RING= no longer determine the loaded map; they no longer exist.
     - [x] A malformed or missing asset fails with a readable error naming the field, not a panic from =Path::build=.
     - [x] The map type is shared by client and server and imports no =lightyear= type.

   *Done, and the board that landed is not a placeholder.* The shipped asset is
   the real board, extracted from the map's own region graph (`map-015`), so the
   constants were not moved — they were replaced by the geometry that the game
   they were approximating actually runs. Four things came with the change and
   are worth keeping in view:

   - The origin is the *middle* of the board. The extracted coordinates are
     Warcraft III world units over a playable =−6144..6144= square, so
     =cell_to_world= / =world_to_cell= are centred and the whole 32x24/edge-at-zero
     assumption is gone. =tests/map.rs= asserts =world_to_cell(goal.centre) ==
     IVec2::new(48, 4)=.
   - A lane *ends*. =Lane::sample= clamps and reports arrival rather than wrapping
     with =rem_euclid=, and =Lane::total= is the end of the lane — which is what
     =sim-006= leaks against.
   - The board is part of the protocol version. =MapData::hash()= is FNV-1a over
     the canonicalised geometry, and =ProtocolVersion= carries it beside the
     balance hash, so a peer with a different board is refused before an entity
     exists (`net-001`, revision 4 of the schema).
   - =config::check_map_against_balance= refuses a board whose =path_clearance=
     the shortest tower cannot reach: a map and a table that make a tower unable
     to hit a lane is a board with no possible defence, not a tuning problem.

* Path geometry

** TODO [#A] Make the funnel genuinely shared   :@map:
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:         map-002
   :EFFORT:     1w
   :END:
   The board has three distinct path shapes — a ring (Lineage A), an open lane
   to a goal (Lineage B), and the funnel the ten lanes merge into — and only the
   first and second are expressible today. The third is the one that costs
   something: in the original a creep is *re-ordered* to the next region when it
   enters the funnel, so ten lanes become one path near the goal. Here each lane
   repeats the funnel in its own polyline, which keeps the visible behaviour (the
   creeps meet and reach the same goal) but leaves the lanes 13k to 32k units
   long, so "furthest along its lane" is only an approximation of "closest to the
   goal" ([[file:02-simulation.org][02-simulation.org]] =sim-010=, =sim-004=).

   - Files: =src/map.rs=, =assets/maps/*.ron=
   - Acceptance:
     - [ ] The funnel is one shared segment (or one shared region) that the lanes re-enter, not a copy per lane, so lane length measures the walk to the funnel plus the funnel.
     - [ ] =sample(dist)= is continuous across a lane's junction with the funnel, and a ring still wraps while a lane-to-goal still reports arrival.
     - [ ] =distance_to(p)= stays correct for a shape that crosses itself (a figure-8 or the funnel's own turns).
     - [ ] With a shared funnel, "least distance remaining" and "furthest along" agree for creeps in different lanes, and =sim-004='s policy is the one the board actually wants.

* Spawns, lanes and build regions

** DONE [#A] Make the board's lanes the spawn set, not the lobby's   :@map:
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:         map-004
   :EFFORT:     1d
   :END:
   Research could not confirm the original's lane count, so the earlier decision
   was one spawn point per player, with =creeps_per_wave()='s connected-player
   multiplier supplying the variety. The real board answered the question instead:
   it has ten spawn regions (nine colours, red holding two so the middle player is
   attacked from both sides) and its wave triggers create units at *every* one of
   them, whether or not a human holds that colour. A solo player therefore faces
   all ten lanes, which is what the original does.

   - Files: =src/sim/waves.rs=, =src/data/balance.rs=, =assets/maps/green_td_v36.ron=
   - Acceptance:
     - [x] The spawn set is the board's lanes; a lane is not owned by a player and a join or leave does not change which lanes exist.
     - [x] A wave is spawned on every lane, so =wave_size = creeps_per_lane × lanes=.
     - [x] The connected-player multiplier stays, now as the *difficulty* multiplier rather than the spawn-set size, and it is documented as one of two multipliers.
     - [x] A creep carries a =lane= index, not a player id, so it can be re-laned by a map change and cannot be mistaken for a player's unit.

   What this leaves is not map work: how a smaller lobby should be *scaled*
   against ten lanes is the difficulty table (=waves-004=) and the lobby
   (=lobby-003=), and the per-lane count is =creeps_per_lane()= in
   =src/data/balance.rs=.

* Open questions

  - *Spawn lane count.* Settled by the board: ten lanes, one spawn each except red's two. A lane is not owned by a player (=map-004=).
  - *Do Lineage A ring creeps loop forever or stop at the ring end?* Default:
    loop forever on a closed ring, so =Overrun= can accumulate. Lineage A still
    has no shipped board (=map-013=).
  - *Exact build-region ownership layout.* The board's zones are decoration and
    the original never reads them, so the default is "build anywhere the lanes
    are clear" (=map-006=, =audit-026=). Anything narrower would be a mechanic
    this project invents.
  - *Real serpentine, multi-lane and figure-8 geometry.* The shipped board's
    lanes are real; a ring board for Lineage A is still author-defined, =(tune)=.
  - *Default spawn direction.* Settled by the board: each lane's first waypoint
    is its spawn and its polyline is the direction (=map-001=).
  - *Does the goal move or multiply per lane?* No: one region per board, =END=,
    and every lane's last waypoint is its centre (=map-003=).
